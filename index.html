03  The box

Opened on real
quantum hardware.

When you open the box, BOX sends one tiny program to IBM Quantum: put your qubit into an equal superposition, then measure it once. Nobody can choose the answer. Not you, not us.

The circuitOpenQASM 3 · IBM Sampler
OPENQASM 3.0;
include "stdgates.inc";
bit[1] c;
rz(pi/2) $42;     // Hadamard, in the chip's
sx $42;           // own native gates:
rz(pi/2) $42;     // |0⟩ → (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2
c[0] = measure $42; // one shot: 0 or 1
0Version A is born
1Version B is born

Before the job runs, BOX tags it at IBM with your wallet, your qubit and a fingerprint of both versions. The measurement can only pick between the two you committed. Each wallet gets one opening per qubit per day: no re-rolls.

07  FAQ

Questions.

Is it really a quantum computer?

Yes. Every box is a job on IBM Quantum Platform, run on ibm_fez or ibm_marrakesh (IBM Heron r2, 156 qubits each). The job puts one physical qubit (the one you claimed) into an equal superposition with a Hadamard gate and measures it once. The result, 0 or 1, decides which version is born. Every coin page shows the IBM job ID and the measured bit.

Can anyone rig the result?

Before the job runs, it is tagged at IBM with your wallet, your qubit and a fingerprint (hash) of both versions, so the measurement can only choose between the two versions you committed. Each wallet gets one opening per qubit per day, so you can't re-roll until you like the answer. The launch transaction is only built for the version the qubit picked.

What does "claiming a qubit" mean?

Your coin is paired with one real physical qubit, for example Q42 on ibm_fez. It's recorded on-chain in your coin's launch transaction, and it's yours: the first coin on a qubit holds it for good. Your coin page shows that qubit's live calibration from IBM: how long it holds a quantum state (T1, T2) and how often it's misread.

Why is every coin paired to $BOX?

Coins born on BOX are priced in $BOX instead of SOL (pump.fun Custom Pairs). Buying any of them goes SOL → $BOX → coin in one transaction, so every buy of a BOX coin is also a buy of $BOX.

Who gets the creator fees?

The coin's holders. Every coin is created with pump.fun Holder Rewards switched on, so 100% of its creator fees go to holders, paid by pump.fun. It is permanent.

What happens to the version that loses?

It never launches. Right after your coin is born you sign one small record that puts the measurement and the other version's name, ticker and picture on-chain. That's the Graveyard.

How big can the dev buy be?

Up to 1 SOL, bought right after the launch through $BOX.

What does it cost?

Launching is free on BOX. You pay pump.fun's normal fees, Solana network fees and a little rent, and every transaction is simulated before your wallet signs it. BOX never holds your keys.
